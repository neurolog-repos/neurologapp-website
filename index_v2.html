The problem

Between appointments, nobody can see what is happening.

People with epilepsy and other episodic neurological conditions are managed primarily within brief, infrequent clinic visits, where clinicians rely on patient recall — subjective, and skewed toward recent events — to interpret treatment response and disease progression.

Between appointments, neurologists have no objective picture of event frequency or treatment efficacy. The patient is left equally in the dark: unable to track their own patterns, and largely dependent on clinic visits for any active engagement with their condition.

Neurolog closes the loop between patient experience and objective biosignals.

For patients — mobile app

Your own record of what is actually happening.

Most of life with an episodic condition happens away from the clinic. Neurolog gives you somewhere to put it — so you can look back at your own patterns, and so the person treating you can see them too.

Log an event in seconds

Record what happened when you are able to — in the moment or later — with only as much detail as you want to give.

Keep medications straight

Track what you take and when it changed, so a change in your events can be read against a change in treatment.

Connect a wearable

If you already wear a device, sync it once and your sleep and activity sit alongside the events you log.

For clinicians — web app

Start the consultation with more than recall.

One summary report per patient, assembled from what they logged and what their device recorded since you last saw them — event frequency and type, sleep health, activity, and how physiology tracked alongside symptoms.

01

Objective event frequency

Counts and types over the whole interval, not the fortnight the patient happens to remember.

02

Treatment response in context

Medication changes sit on the same timeline as events, sleep and activity.

03

No extra work in clinic

The patient does the logging. Your view is read-only and ready when you open it.
